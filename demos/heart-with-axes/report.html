Heart with Axes

The heart shape, but now the axes mean something — Intimacy × Passion.

Chart Brief

  • Big Idea: The heart shape traced onto the Intimacy × Passion plane isn't decoration — the two upper lobes sit in the high-passion quadrants (infatuation to the left, consummate love to the right), the two lower sides sit in the low-passion quadrants (indifference to the left, companionate love to the right), and the curve between them traces the emotional transitions. Love's iconic silhouette is the four-archetype map.
  • Story arc: Relationship (with a decorative locus overlay that reinforces, rather than replaces, the argument)
  • Audience: Read (30s) / General / Persuasive
  • Style stance: INNOVATIVE — the heart-as-locus overlay adds a visual mnemonic on top of the argumentative I × P plane. This is the extra composition earning its cost by giving the reader a memorable image to associate with the two-axis model.
  • chartType: layered connected_scatter_plot — shaded quadrant backgrounds + midpoint reference rules + the heart curve as an ordered line + points at each parametric sample + faint quadrant-region labels
  • Alternates considered: (a) Plain heart curve (previous 2026-07-24-heart-chart/ demo) — chosen against because it argues nothing; kept as a "decorative capability demo" reference. (b) Bare I × P quadrant chart (2026-07-24-love-axes/) — chosen against for this variant because the user explicitly wanted the heart shape retained; kept as the pure-argument reference.
  • Anti-patterns to avoid: don't let the heart's visual weight overwhelm the axes (the axes are the argument, the heart is the mnemonic); don't overload with too many archetype annotations (four is legible, six starts to crowd the shape).

The heart curve is the standard parametric heart (x = 16 sin³(t), y = 13 cos(t) − 5 cos(2t) − 2 cos(3t) − cos(4t)), linearly rescaled to fit centered in the Intimacy × Passion plane. Archetype coordinates are illustrative.
